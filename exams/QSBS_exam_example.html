QSBS Basic statistics example exam (total time: 2 hours)

Instructions

  • You will have two hours to complete this exam.
  • The exam consists of 7 questions. Questions 1–5 require you to carry out computations in R; questions 6–7 are based on R output and figures that are given in the question and require no computations.
  • You may use the QSBS R command reference and the help pages within R. All data sets are in the package qsbsdata; load it with library(qsbsdata) together with MASS.
  • For the R questions, write down your answers (interpretation in words and any values, if requested) and upload your R code with the question. A number without interpretation, or an interpretation without the number it is based on, does not earn full marks.
  • Report numbers with a sensible number of decimals. State clearly which model / test / statistic you used.
  • The points per question are indicated, the total is 20 points.

Question 1 — Descriptive statistics

The data set Swallowtail contains wing measurements of 194 swallowtail butterflies of two subspecies.

a (1 pt)

For the variable Forewing_area (cm²), report the mean, median, standard deviation, interquartile range and the skewness statistic \(\frac{1}{n}\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^3 / s^3\)

b (1 pt)

make a boxplot and give the values of any boxplot outliers. Summarise the distribution in two or three sentences: is it symmetric, and are the outliers a reason for concern?

Question 2 — Association between categorical variables

Using the data set Swallowtail, are the sexes equally represented in the two subspecies (Species)?

a (1 pt)

Make the cross table of Species and Sex, calculate the proportion of females within each subspecies.

b (2 pt)

Test the association with a chi-square test. Report the expected counts under independence, the test statistic, the degrees of freedom and the p-value, and formulate a conclusion in one sentence.

Question 3 — Probability distributions

Use the data set Swallowtail. Suppose that forewing area (Swallowtail$Forewing_area) in this population follows a normal distribution with mean 5.26 cm² and standard deviation 0.73 cm².

a (1 pt)

Calculate (i) the probability that a randomly chosen butterfly has a forewing area larger than 6.5 cm², (ii) the probability that it lies between 4.5 and 6.0 cm², and (iii) the forewing area that is exceeded by only 5 % of the butterflies.

b (1 pt)

Compare answer (i) with the corresponding observed proportion and compare answer (iii) with the quantile in Swallowtail$Forewing_area. Comment on whether the normal distribution is a reasonable description.

Question 4 — GLM interpretation

The data set bolger records for 25 habitat fragments around San Diego whether native rodents were present (RODENTSP, 1 = present, 0 = absent) and the percentage shrub cover of the fragment (PERSHRUB).

a (1 pt)

Fit a GLM that models the presence of rodents as a function of shrub cover. Report the model equation with the estimated coefficients, state the link function.

b (1 pt)

Give the predicted probability of rodent presence at 20 %, 50 % and 80 % shrub cover. By what factor do the odds of rodent presence change for every 10 % increase in shrub cover?

c (2 pt)

Draw the data with the fitted curve and its 95 % confidence band.

Question 5 — GLM comparison

The data set birds contains the bird species richness S in 306 habitat patches in Jamaica together with the patch area (area, ha). Create a new variable in the dataset by birds$logarea <- log(area)

a (1 pt)

Fit the Poisson GLM S ~ logarea. Check the dispersion of this model and refit it with a more appropriate error distribution.

b (1 pt)

Report for both models the estimate and standard error of the slope and explain what the difference in standard errors means for the conclusions.
